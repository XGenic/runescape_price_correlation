0 — A separate historical experiment¶

The 365-day limit belonged to the real-time endpoint used in the original investigation, not to all available bond history. The Weird Gloop archive supplies historical GE guide-price updates. This notebook studies that record separately, then compares it with the transaction-average feed over their overlap. It does not splice the two price definitions or overwrite the first study.

Question: is there a repeatable forward bond-return relationship after accounting for lag search, dependence, conventional controls, missing history, and forecast baselines? A long guide-price archive helps examine earlier regimes but also introduces measurement/update-timing concerns.

Status: retrospective, exploratory research. Training and validation decisions are chronological, but the last historical evaluation includes recent equity dates exposed by our previous study. A new dataset fingerprint and ledger do not make those dates unseen. No result here is an independent confirmatory trading test.

Run Sections 0–1 once per kernel. Later sections recompute their own prerequisites. Source comparison and full-history regime analysis require the historical evaluation report first, so they cannot inform this run's rule selection. Existing notebooks, HTML, fetchers and ledgers remain unchanged.

1 — Audit the archive before using it¶

The archive API stores prices used by the Wiki's historical GE charts. Its documentation identifies GE-update timestamps, not verified first-publication times. The live response uses object records with millisecond Unix timestamps; older documentation shows a different array schema. We use the verified live format and preserve the untouched response plus a raw parquet copy.

Prespecified exclusions and aggregation: discard records dated before the documented 30 March 2015 OSRS bond launch, without assuming those entries were genuine trades. If multiple updates share a UTC date, keep the final timestamp, not their average. Conflicting prices at the exact same timestamp are an audit error. Null volume does not mean zero trades. Keep all gaps visible; never interpolate a price or manufacture a zero return across a missing observation.

A guide quote from UTC day D is conservatively available at D+1 midnight. Unlike the real-time feed, this is an availability convention—not an assertion that the timestamp begins an averaging bucket. Positive one-session forecasts use a value known under this convention before the predicted equity close-to-close period. Historical publication latency and backfills remain unverified.

The original SPX-session alignment function is reused: prices align first, returns are calculated without filling, and missing rows remain on the regular equity calendar. The 60/20/20 boundaries are set before dropping missing values or selecting lags. Raw files are immutable by default in a new cache directory; no date cutoff is chosen to favor a result.

2 — Stationarity without pretending gaps are consecutive days¶

ADF/KPSS require a regular sequence. Dropping scattered missing guide returns before running them would quietly compress time. Instead, diagnose each contiguous training block with at least 60 complete paired sessions. Both price and return series are tested with an intercept and automatic lag/bandwidth selection using the existing arch implementation.

A block passes the return checkpoint only when both assets reject the ADF unit-root null at 5% and neither rejects KPSS level stationarity. Coverage and skipped blocks are reported. Passing a subset of blocks does not confirm stationarity over the entire training history. Unlike the first short study's strict full-contiguous-sample gate, this gapped archive is analyzed conditionally and explicitly as exploratory; missing blocks and failed tests are not made to disappear.

Subsequent regressions and correlations use the full training calendar with valid-row selection after constructing lags, not only the blocks that happen to pass. Stationarity disagreements are retained as limitations rather than used to select a favorable sample.

3 — Search the training lags, then preserve dependence in the null¶

Search −90 through +90 trading sessions, keeping the original calendar and requiring at least 30 finite paired returns at each lag. Positive k means bond returns lead S&P returns; a zero or negative overall winner is not a future-equity predictor. The positive-only winner is selected separately for forecasting and is never assigned the overall winner's significance by implication.

Repeat the entire maximum-absolute-correlation search for 1,000 IID shuffles and 1,000 permutations each of 5- and 20-session blocks. Blocks are formed within contiguous finite bond runs, then shuffled and placed back in the original finite positions. Thus source blocks do not cross missing dates and original missingness remains fixed, but block boundaries still disturb dependence. The block sizes are prespecified sensitivity choices, not tuned to produce a favorable p-value. Use the finite-sample plus-one correction for every whole-search p-value.

A signal should not depend on the most permissive null. These are diagnostics under explicit exchangeability/block assumptions; none repairs revised data, uncertain release timing, or the fact that the later sample is partly exposed.

4 — Conventional controls and predictive direction¶

The controlled training regression uses the strictly positive candidate, previous-session VIX returns, and two-month-delayed monthly UMCSENT changes. It reuses the original HAC(5) implementation and compares controls-only versus augmented R² on identical rows. Delaying latest-vintage sentiment does not restore point-in-time vintages. The lag was selected on the same training outcomes; coefficient significance is post-selection evidence, not a novelty certificate.

Granger tests compare an autoregression with the same model plus all source lags 1 through p, in both directions at orders 1, 5, 10, 30 and the absolute overall winning lag. Lag columns are built on the original session grid before incomplete rows are removed. No day after a gap is relabeled adjacent to an earlier distant day. Classical nested F statistics and Holm p-values across all estimable direction/order combinations are reported. Autocorrelation, heteroskedasticity, selection and uncertain global stationarity limit the classical F inference. Granger predictability is not causation.

5 — A frozen historical evaluation, not a pristine holdout¶

Use the strictly positive lag selected in historical training, never the most favorable lag in validation or the final period. Reuse the earlier walk-forward implementation: initial-training absolute-signal quantiles define no-gate / median / 75th-percentile candidates; validation selects the highest directional accuracy with a deterministic simplest-gate tie rule. Refit an intercept-plus-bond-return OLS every 20 original sessions using strictly preceding eligible outcomes. Below-gate predictions use the majority direction from those same historical fitting rows.

The final chronological 20% is evaluated only after the gate is locked. Earlier outcomes from that period can enter later scheduled refits, never their own batch's fit. Score the signal and matched-majority baseline on the same nonmissing, nonzero-target dates. A separate ledger freezes this run, but its recent dates overlap the previously exposed experiment: call the result historical evaluation, not untouched validation of novelty.

Wilson intervals below are nominal binomial intervals for accuracy, not dependence-adjusted confidence intervals for improvement over a paired baseline. Accuracy is not profitability. Changing models after this evaluation requires a new future-data protocol, not deleting the ledger.

Collective reading¶

The longer guide-price record does not establish a robust novel bond-leading-equity signal. At least one key whole-search, directionality or forecast-baseline check fails; favorable selected-training coefficients cannot override that chain of evidence. The measurement comparison motivates keeping the two feeds separate. Neither study identifies a causal mechanism or establishes a profitable strategy.

Recommended next studies¶

  1. Preregister genuinely future dates. Preserve both experiments and their spent ledgers. Specify a minimum useful paired forecast improvement, evaluation horizon, and dependence-aware uncertainty before observing more outcomes. A new partition of already viewed dates is not fresh confirmation.
  2. Verify guide publication and adjustment mechanics. Investigate midnight-stamped records, multi-update days, historical backfills and guide-price adjustment rules. Collect first-seen timestamps prospectively. Test whether the cross-source lag is stable under alternative defensible availability conventions without tuning the present equity evaluation.
  3. Audit robustness without cherry-picking. Repeat the entire lag search inside dependence-preserving resamples, predeclare block sizes and equal-overlap comparisons, and document model/instrument multiplicity. If an effect disappears under sensible choices, retain the null conclusion.
  4. Use vintage-correct controls and stronger matched baselines. ALFRED/release timestamps, equity autoregressions, rates and FX can test incremental information. Compare baseline versus bonds-plus-controls on the same future outcomes; revised sentiment and in-sample R² do not establish novelty.
  5. Investigate mechanisms with negative controls. Add bond volume/spreads where available from transaction data, player activity, membership-price changes, update dates and an in-game inflation basket. Form explicit predictions that distinguish a game-specific supply shock from a broad macro channel.
  6. Treat regimes as hypotheses. A formal autocorrelation/heteroskedasticity-robust Bai–Perron procedure and breakpoint uncertainty require a separately validated specification. Do not optimize a regime-conditioned rule on the dates used to discover those regimes. Economic usefulness still requires implementable quotes, costs and calibrated return magnitudes.

What would strengthen the claim: a forward effect surviving the whole search and realistic nulls, improving a strong paired baseline on truly new observations, with timing and source differences accounted for. What would weaken it: null maxima as large as the observed peak, equally strong reverse relationships, no future baseline advantage, or apparent predictability explained by guide-update mechanics.
